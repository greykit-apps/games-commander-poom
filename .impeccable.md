## Design Context

### Users
Visitors who wander in from greykit.com's games page. They give it a few minutes at a desk and judge it on first impression: the title screen, the first map, the first level. Many will know Commander Keen, and nobody reads the help first. Most will play the newest Book's opening, so Book One has to land within a minute: what the game is, who Poom is, where to walk, what to jump on.

### Brand Personality
**Period, wry, gentle.** It is a faithful homage to a 1990–91 shareware episode. The joke is that each Book is a mashup: Keen's game, worn in another world's clothes. Book One wears a post-war wasteland in the spirit of *Fallout*. Its names, art and text are its own.

greykit.com's voice is wry, specific and brief. It is never zany and never uses emoji. The world is animals, not people; it is darkly funny, never grim. Nobody dies on screen, and raiders are bullies.

**Emotional goal:** "I remember this." The sense of booting a shareware episode in 1991, then the surprise of its setting.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **The game is the reference.** It should look like the PC platformers of 1990–92:
  - a 320×200 canvas, pixel art scaled with no smoothing
  - a chunky pixel font (Press Start 2P)
  - boxed menus and message windows
  - a scorebox in the corner
  - the PC speaker, or Sound Blaster-era sound
- **Each Book themes everything**, and the game dresses as the last Book played:
  - Poom's look, the tilesets, the creatures and the items
  - the world map and the title scene
  - the menus and message boxes
  - the scorebox and the sound
  - the words: story, signs, messages, place names
- **Book One, the Glow:**
  - full VGA colour
  - a dusk sky, rust and amber
  - concrete and steel Vaults, frozen in Vault 9
  - scrap-metal raider camps, ruined towns
  - rusted-steel menu panels with amber lettering
  - a green-phosphor wrist-computer scorebox
- **The classic look** (EGA blue boxes, Planet Hiss) is kept in the code, unused.
- **Anti-references:**
  - modern UI idioms: soft shadows, rounded cards, glass, gradients for show
  - smooth vector art
  - anything that reads as a 2020s indie "pixel-styled" game rather than a 1991 one
  - the IP being parodied: no Vault-Tec, Pip-Boy, Nuka-Cola or deathclaws
- **No build, no dependencies, offline at play time.** All art is drawn in code (`js/art.js`).
- **Desktop first.** Judge it with a keyboard or gamepad at desktop size. Touch must work, but is secondary.

### Design Principles
1. **Period authenticity wins.** When a modern convention and a 1991 one conflict, take 1991, unless it makes the game unplayable or unreadable.
2. **The playfield is the hero.** The HUD, the logo and the messages frame play and get out of the way. The title card fades; the scorebox stays small.
3. **Teach in the world.** Signs, pickups and first-time messages explain the game where it happens. The help is for looking things up, not for learning.
4. **Theme everything, together.** A Book's look, words and sound change as one. A screen still in another Book's clothes is a bug.
5. **Readable, best effort.** Text must be legible and the game keyboard-playable. Accessibility beyond that is welcome, but doesn't override the period look.
